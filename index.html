Why Hospitality Demands a Specialist

A restaurant is not a generic business with a kitchen attached. It runs on high transaction volumes, perishable inventory that loses value by the hour, heavy cash handling, shift-based and tipped labor, and revenue split across dine-in, takeaway, catering and a changing roster of delivery aggregators. A generalist records all of this after the fact; a specialist manages the levers that decide whether a concept survives its second year.

Our F&B practice is organized around prime cost, the combined weight of food, beverage and labor, because it is the single figure that most reliably predicts a venue's survival. We track it against sales on a weekly rhythm rather than waiting for year-end, so problems surface while there is still time to fix them.

Key Areas for Operating in Lebanon

Choosing a Business Structure

Lebanon offers several vehicles, from the SAL joint-stock company and the SARL limited-liability company to holding and offshore structures. The right choice depends on where your income arises, who your partners are, and where you bank.

Holding Company Structures: Tax Optimization Strategies → Offshore Incorporation: Lebanon vs. Cyprus →

Corporate Tax and the Annual Budget

Corporate income tax, its brackets and its filing calendar are set by the Ministry of Finance and revised through the annual budget and its decrees. Because rates and deadlines change, plan against the current rules and file within the windows, which tend to tighten.

2026 Budget Analysis: Navigating New Corporate Tax Brackets → Mid-Year 2026 Compliance Review: Tax & NSSF Checkpoints →

VAT and the Shift to E-Invoicing

Value Added Tax applies to most goods and services, with specific rules on registration, filing and recovery. The region is also moving toward mandatory electronic invoicing, which is a data and systems project as much as a tax one.

VAT Recovery on Bad Debts in Lebanon → The Shift to E-Invoicing: Preparing for Digital VAT →

Payroll and Social Security (NSSF)

Employers must register staff with the National Social Security Fund and declare contributions against ceilings the authorities adjust over time. Getting the base and timing right, especially when salaries are partly in foreign currency, is where most payroll risk sits.

NSSF Compliance in a Hyper-Inflationary Economy →

Real Estate and Property

Property remains a preferred store of value, but registration fees, built-property tax and the treatment of gains reward planning at acquisition rather than at sale, and contracts that state currency and payment method clearly.

Navigating the Real Estate Tax Landscape →

Financial Reporting and Audit (IFRS)

Lebanese companies report under IFRS, and the framework is evolving: IFRS 18 reshapes how the income statement is presented, while IFRS 19 eases disclosure for eligible subsidiaries. Statutory audit requirements apply to many entities.

The Transition to IFRS 18: What MENA Enterprises Need to Know → IFRS 19: Simplified Disclosures for Eligible Subsidiaries →

Banking and Trapped Deposits

The banking sector's constraints affect how companies hold cash, value legacy deposits and move money across borders. Trapped deposits in particular need a defensible accounting treatment under IFRS 9 rather than being carried at their old value.

Accounting for Deposit Recovery Under the Financial Gap Law →

New P&L Categories

Cryptocurrency Asset Valuation

Published: January 29, 2025
← Back to News
Category By the ESO Practice

Executive Summary

Auditors are grappling with how to correctly value digital assets under IFRS.

Key Adjustments & Impact

IFRS currently considers cryptocurrencies as "intangible assets" rather than cash equivalents.

ESO Recommendations

Corporate treasuries must maintain meticulous logs of cold wallet addresses and transaction hashes.

The Bottom Line

For audit purposes, crypto is not cash; under current IFRS it is usually an intangible asset, and that classification drives how you measure it, when you recognise impairment, and what you must disclose. Treasuries that keep meticulous wallet and transaction records make the audit possible; those that cannot evidence ownership and history make it very hard to give an opinion.

Source & Reference: IFRIC Interpretation on Holdings of Cryptocurrencies and IAS 38 (Intangible Assets).

Category By the ESO Practice

Executive Summary

Following IFRS 18, the IASB has issued IFRS 19 for eligible subsidiaries.

Key Adjustments & Impact

A subsidiary qualifies if it has no public accountability and its parent reports under IFRS.

ESO Recommendations

We recommend mapping eligible subsidiaries now, ahead of the 2027 effective date.

The Bottom Line

IFRS 19 is a chance to cut disclosure work without leaving the IFRS framework. Eligible subsidiaries, those without public accountability whose parent reports under IFRS, can apply reduced disclosures while keeping full IFRS recognition and measurement. Groups that map eligibility now can lighten every qualifying subsidiary's reporting at the next year-end.

Source & Reference: Based on IFRS 19 'Subsidiaries without Public Accountability: Disclosures', as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB).

Deposit Recovery & the Financial Gap Law

Published: May 15, 2026
← Back to News
Category By the ESO Practice

Executive Summary

Enterprises holding trapped bank deposits must decide at what value to carry them.

Key Adjustments & Impact

Under IFRS 9, these deposits are financial assets subject to expected credit loss assessment.

ESO Recommendations

We advise documenting a defensible impairment model tied to published recovery terms.

The Bottom Line

Trapped deposits sit awkwardly on a balance sheet: still recorded at their old value, but worth a fraction in practice. Under IFRS 9 the honest answer is a documented impairment, an expected-credit-loss view tied to whatever recovery framework is actually enacted, and clear disclosure of the assumptions. A defensible model beats both denial and guesswork.

Source & Reference: Analysis under IFRS 9 (Financial Instruments) in the context of published guidance on Lebanon's banking-sector recovery framework.
